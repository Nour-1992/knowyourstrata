What a valid requisition needs

    The clock

      Changing December 31, 2026

      The 15% stays. The process around it is rebuilt.

      A requisition the board receives on or after December 31, 2026 runs under the new section 46. One received before that day stays under the old section to the end (O. Reg. 48/01 s. 80).

      • A new eligibility bar: requisitionists cannot have common expense contributions 30 days or more in arrears.
      • A prescribed form, the Requisition to Call and Hold a Meeting of Owners, replaces a plain signed letter.
      • The board has 20 days to respond in writing. Silence counts as agreeing.
      • If it agrees, the meeting must be held within 40 days after the response period ends, about 60 days from receipt instead of 35.
      • If it declines, requisitionists have 10 days to revise, or 20 days to apply to the Condominium Authority Tribunal or the Superior Court of Justice. The “call it yourself” option ends.

      Condominium Act, 1998, s. 46 as replaced by 2015, c. 28, Sched. 1, s. 40; O. Reg. 48/01, ss. 12.2.1 to 12.2.7 and 80, as amended by O. Reg. 236/26. Enter a received date on or after December 31, 2026 to see the new clock.

      Two versions of section 46, and the date that picks one

      Section 46 of the Condominium Act, 1998 is repealed and replaced on December 31, 2026 by the Protecting Condominium Owners Act, 2015. The changeover is decided by one fact: the day the board received the requisition.

      “Section 46 of the Act, as it reads on the day this section comes into force, does not apply with respect to a requisition that is received by the board before that day and instead section 46 of the Act, as it read immediately before that day, continues to apply to such a requisition.”

      That is O. Reg. 48/01 s. 80. A requisition received on December 30 runs on the old 35 day clock even if the meeting lands in February. One received on December 31 runs on the new process from the start. This calculator applies whichever version the received date calls for.

      Who can requisition

      Both versions set the same bar: owners who own at least 15 per cent of the units, and who appear in the corporation’s s. 46.1 record of owners. The replacement adds a third condition:

      “(c) have no contributions to the common expenses payable for their units that have been in arrears for 30 days or more.”

      Which units count. O. Reg. 48/01 s. 1.1 reads “15 per cent of the units” as 15 per cent of the units that are not parking, storage or service units under s. 49(3) of the Act, unless every unit is one. It applies to the old s. 46(1) and to the new s. 46(1)(a) alike. A corporation with 200 suites and 150 parking units needs owners of 30 units, not 53.

      Reserved board positions. If the business is removing or electing a director in a position reserved under s. 51(6), the 15 per cent is taken of those reserved units instead: the owner-occupied units under O. Reg. 48/01 s. 1.1(1)(a) for the old section, and the non-leased voting units under the new s. 46(2). A requisition that fails that test but meets the general one can still go ahead for its other business (new s. 46(3)). Choose the reserved position in the calculator above and enter how many units hold the reserved vote to get the number.

      The old section: 35 days, then 45

      For a requisition received before December 31, 2026, it must be in writing, signed by the requisitionists, state the nature of the business, and be delivered personally or by registered mail to the president or secretary, or deposited at the corporation’s address for service (s. 46(2)). If it seeks to remove directors it must name each one, give the reasons, and say whether the director holds a reserved position (s. 46(3)).

      The board must then either add the business to the next AGM, if the requisitionists ask or consent in writing, or “call and hold a meeting of owners within 35 days” (s. 46(4)). If it does neither, a requisitionist may call the meeting, to be held within 45 days of the day it is called (s. 46(5)), and the corporation must reimburse the reasonable costs on request (s. 46(6)).

      The new section: respond in 20, hold within 40 more

      • The form. The requisition must contain the prescribed information in the prescribed form (new s. 46(5)): the Requisition to Call and Hold a Meeting of Owners, published by the Condominium Authority of Ontario (O. Reg. 48/01 ss. 12.2.1 and 16.1). It names the corporation, the unit count and what 15 per cent of it is, each requisitionist’s unit, the purpose, and one or two contacts for the board’s reply.
      • Delivery. Personally, by registered mail, or at the address for service (new s. 46(6)), and also by prepaid mail, courier or the mail box at the corporation’s or the condominium manager’s address for service or an address the board has named by resolution, or by email or other electronic means if the board has accepted that by resolution (O. Reg. 48/01 s. 12.2.2).
      • Purpose. An information meeting with no vote beyond routine procedure, the removal or election of directors, or another purpose the Act or regulations allow (new s. 46(4)).
      • The board responds in 20 days. The Act says “within 10 days or such other time period, if any, that is prescribed”, and O. Reg. 48/01 s. 12.2.3 prescribes 20. The response is in writing and either agrees to call the meeting or says why the requisition does not comply (new s. 46(7)). Silence counts as agreeing (new s. 46(10)).
      • The meeting within 40 more days. Once the board agrees, or is deemed to, it must call and hold the meeting within 40 days after the end of the 20 day response period, or add the business to an AGM held at least 40 days after it if the requisitionists asked for that (new s. 46(11)). From receipt, that is about 60 days.
      • If the board declines. The requisitionists may revise within 10 days on a fresh form (new s. 46(13); O. Reg. 48/01 s. 12.2.7), or within 20 days apply to the Condominium Authority Tribunal, or to the Superior Court of Justice where the Tribunal cannot hear it. Otherwise the requisition is deemed abandoned (new s. 46(15)). The old right to call the meeting yourself does not carry over.
      • Withdrawal. The named contact can withdraw the requisition by written notice within 15 days after delivering it, and the board then need not respond (new s. 46(8); O. Reg. 48/01 s. 12.2.4).

      Counting the days

      Each deadline is counted the way the Legislation Act, 2006 counts “within” a number of days: excluding the day of the trigger, so day 1 is the day after it. If the last day falls on a holiday, s. 89(1) moves it to the next day that is not one. Sunday is a holiday under s. 88(2); Saturday is not, so a deadline landing on a Saturday stays there. A holiday fixed by proclamation cannot be predicted, so check any date that lands near one.

      Do parking and locker units count toward the 15 per cent?
      No, when they are separate units. O. Reg. 48/01 s. 1.1 reads “15 per cent of the units” in s. 46(1) as 15 per cent of the units that are not parking, storage or service units under s. 49(3) of the Act. The same rule carries over to the replacement s. 46(1)(a) on December 31, 2026. A corporation with 200 suites and 150 parking units needs 30 units, not 53. Enter them in the second box and the calculator does this for you.
      Our board received a requisition on December 20, 2026. Which rules apply?
      The old ones, all the way through. O. Reg. 48/01 s. 80 keeps the old s. 46 for any requisition the board received before December 31, 2026, so the board still has 35 days to call and hold the meeting, and the 45 day self-help route under the old s. 46(5) is still there if it does not.
      Does the 15 per cent threshold change on December 31, 2026?
      No. The figure in the new s. 46(1)(a) is the same as in the old s. 46(1), and O. Reg. 48/01 s. 1.1 applies to both. What changes is who is eligible, the form, and the whole timeline.
      The Act says the board has 10 days to respond. Why does this page say 20?
      Because the new s. 46(7) says “within 10 days or such other time period, if any, that is prescribed”, and O. Reg. 48/01 s. 12.2.3 prescribes 20 days. The regulation was amended for this by O. Reg. 236/26 and comes into force on the same day.
      What if the board never answers?
      Under the new section, silence is a yes. New s. 46(10) deems a board that does not respond in time to have agreed, and the 40 day clock to hold the meeting starts when the 20 day response period ends.
      Is December 31, 2026 a fixed date?
      It is the day named by order of the Lieutenant Governor in Council, as printed in the official consolidation on e-Laws. Ontario has moved condominium proclamation dates before, so this page is checked against e-Laws every week and will say so if the date moves.
      Is this legal advice?
      No. It is general information based on the legislation, verified against the sources linked above. For anything contested, especially a declined requisition heading to the Tribunal, talk to a condominium lawyer.
