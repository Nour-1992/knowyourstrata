Part 1

The Compliance Calendar

The recurring obligations a board has to hit, in one place. Each one cites the section it rests on and the free tool that does the math.

Preliminary Notice & Notice of Meeting

35 days, combined

The Notice of Meeting must be given at least 15 days before the day of the meeting. Before that notice can go out, a Preliminary Notice must be given at least 20 days before it. For any board-called meeting — an AGM, a turn-over meeting, or a meeting called for other business — that means the Preliminary Notice needs to go out at least 35 days ahead of the meeting date. Neither figure changes by meeting type.

Condominium Act, 1998, ss. 47(1)(b)–(c)

Source: knowyourstrata.com/on/notice-of-meeting-deadline — verified 19 August 2026

Owner-requisitioned meeting

35 days, or 45 as fallback

Owners holding at least 15% of the units may requisition a meeting. Once the board receives a valid requisition, it must call and hold the meeting within 35 days (or add the business to the next AGM, if the requisitionists agree). If the board doesn't act, a requisitionist may call the meeting themselves, to be held within 45 days of doing so. The 15-day/20-day notice minimums above still apply to this meeting too.

Condominium Act, 1998, ss. 46(1), 46(4)(b), 46(5)

Changing December 31, 2026: the 15% threshold stays the same, but the process around it doesn't — a new arrears eligibility bar, a prescribed form, and a restructured timeline running to roughly 60 days total rather than 35. See Part 2's requisition checklist and Part 3's tracker for the detail.

Source: knowyourstrata.com/on/requisition-threshold and /on/notice-of-meeting-deadline — verified 20 & 19 August 2026

Records request — board's response

30 days

The board must determine whether to allow access and respond to the requester in writing within 30 days of receiving a request for records. That response is the board's answer, not the record itself — delivery of the record runs on a separate clock, depending on whether it's a core record and how it's being delivered: the same 30 days if it's a core record delivered electronically, 7 days after payment if a core record on paper, or 30 days after payment for anything else.

O. Reg. 48/01, s. 13.3(6); ss. 13.4–13.7

Source: knowyourstrata.com/on/records-request-deadline — verified 19 August 2026

Reserve fund study

1 year, then every 3

The first reserve fund study is due within a year of registering the declaration and description, and must be a comprehensive study. Every study after that is due within 3 years of the previous study's completion — a rolling clock measured from the last study, not a fixed calendar date. The class required alternates between an updated study with and without a site inspection; it does not loop back to a full comprehensive study on any set cycle.

Condominium Act, 1998, s. 94(4) · O. Reg. 48/01, ss. 31(2)–(4)

Source: knowyourstrata.com/on/reserve-fund-study-deadline — verified 20 August 2026

After the reserve fund study lands

120 → 15 → 30 days

Once the board receives the study, it has 120 days to review it and propose a funding plan. From there, 15 more days to notify owners (a summary) and the auditor (the full study and plan). Then 30 more days after that notice before the board must implement the plan.

Condominium Act, 1998, s. 94(8)–(10)

Source: knowyourstrata.com/on/reserve-fund-study-deadline — verified 20 August 2026

Part 4

New Board Member Orientation

A plain-English walk-through of the job's recurring obligations. Every specific figure here is the same one cited in Part 1 — this section doesn't introduce anything new, it just explains it.

Serving on an Ontario condo board mostly means running the same few obligations on repeat, on time, in the right order. The Compliance Calendar in Part 1 is the actual reference; this section is the plain-English version of why each entry matters.

Notice comes in two parts, not one

Before the board can send the Notice of Meeting itself, it has to send a Preliminary Notice first — and that Preliminary Notice needs its own 20-day runway before the Notice of Meeting goes out, which then needs another 15 days before the meeting. Miss the sequencing and the whole 35-day chain slips.

Do the math: knowyourstrata.com/on/notice-of-meeting-deadline

Quorum is a share of units, and the reduced number is narrower than it sounds

Quorum is 25% of the units by default. Only an AGM or a turn-over meeting gets the reduced 15% figure, and only on the third attempt — a requisitioned meeting or anything else stays at a flat 25% no matter how many times it's tried. It's easy to assume the 15% number is more general than it actually is.

Do the math: knowyourstrata.com/on/quorum

An owner requisition has its own threshold and its own clock — and that clock is about to change

Owners holding 15% of the units can force a meeting; the board then has 35 days to hold it, or a requisitionist can step in and call it themselves within 45 more days. That 15% figure is confirmed to survive Ontario's pending December 31, 2026 amendments unchanged — but almost everything else about the process doesn't. Check the date before running this one on autopilot.

Do the math: knowyourstrata.com/on/requisition-threshold

Records requests run on two clocks, not one

The board's 30-day response is just the first step — it says yes or no and states the fee. Actually delivering the record runs on a second clock that depends on whether it's a "core record" (a short, fixed list) and how it's delivered. Treating the 30 days as the whole obligation is the most common way this one gets missed.

Do the math: knowyourstrata.com/on/records-request-deadline

The reserve fund study cycle rolls forward from the last study, and alternates in class

After the first (always comprehensive) study, each later one is due 3 years after the previous study's completion — not a fixed date on the calendar. The class alternates between two types of "updated" study and never cycles back to a full comprehensive study on its own. A "comprehensive study every six years" rule doesn't actually exist in the text.

Do the math: knowyourstrata.com/on/reserve-fund-study-deadline

Ontario condominium law changes in stages — always check the date

More than one figure in this pack is confirmed current as of the dates in Part 1, with a known future change already on the books (the December 31, 2026 requisition amendments). This pack gives you the law as verified on those dates; confirm anything time-sensitive against the current text before relying on it for something that matters.
