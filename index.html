Send notice no later than Friday, September 25, 2026

Not just a date — the derivation. 14 days of notice, plus 2 for the clear-day counting rule, plus 4 because notice sent by mail or email is deemed received four days later. Councils that count 14 days on a calendar send notice late.

Pick your province

The statutes are different, so the tools are separate. Nothing is shared between them except the discipline.

BC British Columbia Strata Property Act, SBC 1998, c. 43 Six tools — AGM notice, special levies, quorum, voting thresholds, records deadlines, strata fees. ON Ontario Condominium Act, 1998, S.O. 1998, c. 19 One tool so far — quorum for meetings of owners. More being read now.

More provinces coming. Each one waits until its statute has actually been read, section by section, rather than adapted from a neighbouring province's rules. That is slower, and it is the whole point.

Why you can forward these to your board

Compliance answers are only worth anything if you can show where they came from. These tools are built to a single rule: nothing is published until its section has been read from the official consolidation — bclaws.gov.bc.ca for British Columbia, ontario.ca for Ontario — not from memory, and not from a summary site.

  • Every figure carries the exact subsection it rests on, down to s. 108(2)(b) rather than a vague "section 108".
  • Every page shows the date its law was last checked, and the consolidation that text was current to.
  • Where the law is genuinely unsettled, the tool says so — the 20-versus-21-day notice count is shown as a real disagreement, not resolved quietly.
  • Where your own bylaws can override the Act, the answer tells you which rule is which.
