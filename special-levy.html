Two lawful ways to split a levy — and the vote each one needs

Under section 108 of the Strata Property Act, a strata calculates each lot's share of a special levy one of two ways. The standard method is by unit entitlement (section 99), approved by a 3/4 vote. A strata may instead use any method that is a fair division of expenses for that levy — including equal shares — but that route requires a unanimous vote (section 108(2)(b)).

So unit entitlement is not the only legal answer; it is the one that carries on a 3/4 vote, which is why it is what most stratas use and what this calculator applies by default. The formula divides costs by entitlement: each lot's share is its own entitlement number divided by the total entitlement of every lot in the strata plan, applied to the total levy. It is rarely an equal split, and it is never a per-person or per-vote split.

Unit entitlement numbers come from the Schedule of Unit Entitlement filed at the Land Title Office under section 246 — usually a fixed number tied to each lot's size or type, set when the strata plan was registered. Boards should pull these from the strata plan or the strata's records, not estimate them.

Two practical consequences. If your levy passed on a 3/4 vote, it has to be the section 99 entitlement split — a 3/4 vote cannot authorise an equal split unless the entitlements happen to be equal. And if the meeting minutes describe some other division, that division is only good if the resolution passed unanimously. Either way, check your own bylaws and the minutes before relying on the numbers here.

Can our strata split a levy equally instead of by entitlement?
Yes, but it takes a unanimous vote under s. 108(2)(b) — an equal split is a "fair division," not the s. 99 formula. The exception: if every lot's entitlement is already identical, the s. 99 result is an equal split, and a 3/4 vote carries it. Check how your levy was actually approved before using any other split.
What has to be written into the resolution itself?
Five things, under s. 108(3): the purpose, the total amount, the method used to determine each lot's share, each lot's share, and the payment date or instalment dates. A levy with correct arithmetic can still be challenged if the resolution omits one of them.
Where do we find each unit's entitlement number?
On the Schedule of Unit Entitlement filed with the strata plan at the Land Title Office (SPA s. 246), or in your strata's council/AGM package — it doesn't change unless the strata plan is amended.
What if our strata is divided into sections?
Sections can have separate expense schedules and voting groups. Confirm which owners the levy actually applies to before running the total through this calculator — it assumes one flat pool of units sharing one levy.
Why doesn't the total split evenly to the cent?
Dividing by unit entitlement almost never lands on a round number. This calculator rounds every unit to the cent and puts any leftover cent(s) on the unit with the largest entitlement, so the column always adds up to the exact levy approved.
Is this legal or financial advice?
No. It's general information based on the current legislation, verified against the sources linked above. For a contested apportionment, talk to a strata lawyer or your strata manager.
