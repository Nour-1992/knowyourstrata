Part 1

The Compliance Calendar

The recurring obligations a board has to hit, in one place. Each one cites the section it rests on and the free tool that does the math.

AGM / SGM notice

~20 days before

Notice must go out at least 20 days before the meeting when sent by mail, email, fax, or left at the door — 16 days if handed directly to the person. That figure is 14 days of statutory notice, plus 2 for the "at least" counting rule, plus 4 for deemed receipt where notice isn't hand-delivered.

Strata Property Act ss. 45(1), 61(3) · Interpretation Act s. 25

Source: knowyourstrata.com/bc/agm-notice — verified 12 August 2026

Budget approval

at the AGM

The strata's budget for the coming fiscal year is approved by a resolution passed by majority vote at the annual general meeting. If it's voted down, a replacement budget goes to a special general meeting, also decided by majority vote.

Strata Property Act s. 103

Source: knowyourstrata.com/bc/voting-threshold — verified 15 August 2026

CRF contribution — the 10% floor

set with the budget

The annual contribution to the contingency reserve fund must be at least 10% of the amount budgeted for the operating fund that year — a flat floor, with no exception for a large existing CRF balance. In the fiscal year following the strata's first AGM the figure comes from Regulation s. 3.4; every year after that, from s. 6.1, which also requires the board to have considered the most recent depreciation report, if any, when setting the amount.

Strata Property Regulation ss. 3.4, 6.1

Source: knowyourstrata.com/bc/crf-contribution — verified 20 August 2026

Records-request response

1–2 weeks

A request for records or documents must be answered within 2 weeks. A request limited to the bylaws or rules, or a request for an Information Certificate (Form B), must be answered within 1 week. The clock runs from the date the request is received — a straight calendar count, with no deemed-receipt or clear-day adjustment.

Strata Property Act ss. 36(3), 59(1)

Source: knowyourstrata.com/bc/records-deadline — verified 15 August 2026

Depreciation report & electrical planning report

dates set by regulation

The Act itself requires a strata corporation to obtain both a depreciation report and an electrical planning report from a qualified person, "on or before the dates determined in accordance with the regulations." The Act does not state a fixed interval in its own text — the actual due dates and cycle are set by the Strata Property Regulation, which has been amended before and can be again.

Strata Property Act ss. 94, 94.1

Verified fresh for this pack on 18 August 2026. The depreciation report deadline tool is now live — use it for your building's dates rather than treating any specific calendar year as fixed, since the regulation has been amended before and can be again.

Special levy & CRF spending — the vote to use

before you spend

A special levy split by unit entitlement needs a 3/4 vote; splitting it any other way — including equally — needs a unanimous vote. Spending from the contingency reserve fund needs only a majority vote for a short list of specific purposes (a depreciation report, EV charging infrastructure, an electrical planning report, or repairs the current depreciation report recommends); everything else needs a 3/4 vote.

Strata Property Act ss. 108(2)(a)–(b), 96(b)(i)(A)–(B)

Source: knowyourstrata.com/bc/voting-threshold and /bc/special-levy — verified 15 & 13 August 2026

Part 4

New Treasurer Orientation

A plain-English walk-through of the job's recurring obligations. Every specific figure here is the same one cited in Part 1 — this section doesn't introduce anything new, it just explains it.

Being strata treasurer mostly means running the same few obligations on repeat, on time, in the right order. The Compliance Calendar in Part 1 is the actual reference; this section is the plain-English version of why each entry matters.

Notice comes first

Nothing else on this list matters if the meeting itself wasn't properly called. Get notice out at least 20 days ahead (16 if you're hand-delivering), and resolutions passed at a badly-noticed meeting are open to challenge no matter how the vote went.

Do the math: knowyourstrata.com/bc/agm-notice

The budget is a majority vote, not a formality

The board proposes it, but the owners approve it by majority vote at the AGM. If it doesn't pass, there's a process for a replacement — it doesn't just lapse.

Do the math: knowyourstrata.com/bc/voting-threshold

Fees follow unit entitlement, not headcount

Once the budget is approved, each unit's share is set automatically by its unit entitlement — not an equal split, not based on how many people live there. A strata can only use a different formula by unanimous vote, filed at the Land Title Office.

Do the math: knowyourstrata.com/bc/strata-fees

A special levy is a different vote, and a specific resolution

Unlike regular fees, a special levy needs its own resolution — and that resolution has to state five specific things or it's vulnerable even if the arithmetic is right. See Part 3's levy resolution template.

Do the math: knowyourstrata.com/bc/special-levy

Records requests run on two different clocks

Most requests get 2 weeks. Bylaws, rules, and Information Certificates (Form B) only get 1. Identify which one you're on before you calculate a deadline.

Do the math: knowyourstrata.com/bc/records-deadline

Quorum is a share of votes, not a headcount of people

You need 1/3 of the strata's total votes present or by proxy (2/3 in a very small strata) — measured against the whole strata's votes, not just who shows up.

Do the math: knowyourstrata.com/bc/quorum

Your bylaws can change some of this — always check them

Quorum and some vote thresholds apply "subject to the bylaws." A registered bylaw can set a different rule than the Act's default. This pack gives you the Act's default in every case; your strata's own registered bylaws govern where they differ.

Part 5

Insurance Deductible — Who Pays

A plain-English summary of the framework, not the full branching walkthrough — the live tool below asks a few questions and routes you to the answer for your specific situation.

When there's a claim on the strata's own insurance policy, the default is that the corporation pays the deductible — it's a common expense, contributed to through strata fees, the same as any other common expense.

Strata Property Act s. 158(1)

What can shift that cost onto an individual owner is a separate, discretionary right: the corporation may sue an owner to recover the deductible if that owner is "responsible" for the loss. Case law reads "responsible" as a materially lower bar than negligence — a loss that simply originated in an owner's unit can be enough, even where the owner did nothing wrong and had no way of knowing anything was faulty. That reading comes from Mari v. Strata Plan LMS 2835, 2007 BCSC 740, reached independently again in Wawanesa Mutual Ins. Co. v. Keiran, 2007 BCSC 727, and affirmed once more in Yang v. Re/Max Commercial Realty Associates (482258 BC Ltd.), 2016 BCSC 2147.

Strata Property Act s. 158(2) · Mari, 2007 BCSC 740 · Wawanesa v. Keiran, 2007 BCSC 727 · Yang, 2016 BCSC 2147

This is the framework only — which path actually applies depends on where a loss originated and whether it stayed contained, and a registered bylaw can change the standard the corporation has to meet. Work through your specific situation with the full tool: knowyourstrata.com/bc/insurance-deductible
